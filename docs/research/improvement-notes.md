# Improvement agenda and open questions

The historical classroom reporting describes engagement and concerns, not a controlled estimate of durable learning. Related studies inform this distinct prototype; they do not establish that it works.

| Learning problem | Pilot response | Review question |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Recognizing a manipulative style does not establish a fact. | Compare the original source, evidence, timing, corroboration and unresolved claims. | Can learners justify the difference between unverified and false? |
| Multiple reposts can trace to one observation. | Let students compare reports and identify dependence. | Do learners transfer that check to a new context? |
| Binary feedback can hide uncertainty and judgment. | Use publish, qualify, hold and explicit repair; feedback explains why each evidence choice does or does not support the claim. | Can more than one responsible action work when justified? |
| Public attention is not success. | Show anchored consequences for readers and accountability; no popularity meter. | Is the story meaningful without rewarding amplification? |
| Debrief can be skipped or confused with a postgame score. | Make it part of the episode and include a brief independent transfer question. | Can students explain what they would check next and why? |

## Not yet decided

Default actor can change after the pilot. Compare an editor, a citizen or a different perspective only when observation says it is worth it. Other meaningful owner decisions: episode length, audience/reading level, reusable assessment without tracking, timing and tone of consequences, later scenario authoring/licensing. Do not add those systems before the pilot gives a reason.

Use a short class trial with observations and new balanced true/misleading/uncertain cases. Compare learning to engagement separately. No personal data in the game.
